## 4. THE $275M QUESTION — READ THIS BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE

The program assumes **$275M tax-exempt**. The financed property is hotel,
retail, entertainment, sports academy, resort and commercial space, owned and
operated by a **private developer**.

Tax-exempt treatment follows the **use of the financed property**, not who
issues the paper. Private business use of that kind is generally not
tax-exempt eligible absent a governmental issuer financing a qualifying public
purpose, or a 501(c)(3) borrower. Neither is established — **no conduit issuer
has even been identified.**

Modeled coupons: tax-exempt 4.75%–5.75%. Taxable 144A 7.25%–11.5%.

**If we are right, a large part of this stack is mispriced by 250–575 basis
points.** If we are wrong, we are about to tell a client to restructure
something that did not need it.

**This is a bond counsel question and it must be answered before we publish a
capital stack.** Finding it ourselves is worth more to the relationship than
any fee on this list.

**One correction to our own engine, for the record:** preflight classifies
"no conduit issuer identified" as NO_GO. Running the pathway showed that is
too harsh — restructuring fully taxable always exists. It changes the deal and
costs 250–575 bp, but it is not a dead end. We caught it in a test and
documented it rather than quietly changing the severity.

## 8. EQUITY — JOSH, THIS SECTION IS FOR YOU

The Equity Grant Letter as drafted has **two defects that can cost NEST cash.**

### 8.1 It sits behind everything, in a structure built not to distribute

The grant is a **profits interest under Rev. Proc. 93-27** in the Project SPV.
It pays only from residual cash — after debt service, after the rate covenant,
after DSRF replenishment, and only when the SPV actually **distributes**.

Garrick's prospectus says the program recycles residual equity: *"$912M+ in
free equity cycles through phases,"* *"Phase 1 stabilization releases equity to
fund Phase 2."* **A program engineered to reinvest residual cash is a program
engineered not to distribute.** A fully vested profits interest here produces
no cash, plausibly for 15+ years. It is a long-dated option on terminal value,
not deferred compensation.

**At 75% LTC this gets worse, not better.** Higher leverage means more of the
cash flow is committed to debt service before anything reaches residual —
which is precisely where a profits interest sits.

### 8.2 Phantom income — we would owe cash tax on money we never receive

Written into the template. Section 1.2 adopts Rev. Proc. 2001-43, under which
NEST is treated as owner of the **entire** grant — explicitly **including the
unvested portion** — and must take into account its distributive share of
Company income in computing tax liability. Section 1.3 requires a protective
§83(b) election.

Once the SPV allocates taxable income to NEST, **NEST owes cash tax on income
it has not received.**

**Do not sign without a mandatory tax distribution provision:**

> The Company shall distribute to each holder of a Granted Interest, no later
> than 75 days following the close of each fiscal year, an amount not less
> than the product of (a) the net taxable income allocated to such holder and
> (b) the highest combined marginal federal and state rate applicable to such
> holder. Such distributions shall be senior in priority to any reinvestment,
> capital recycling, or phase-funding application of Company cash.

This must go in **both** operating agreements — the SPV and the program parent.

### 8.3 The blended structure to propose

| Tranche | Entity | Size | Vests on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project | Phase SPV | 1.5–2.5% profits interest | That phase's development milestones (1/3, 1/3, 1/3) |
| Program | 73 Holdings parent | 0.5–1.0% profits interest | **Time**, 36 months from DSA signing — not on any closing |

Time-vesting at the program level is the cleanest position we can hold while
unlicensed. Time-vested equity for continuous advisory services is the hardest
thing to recharacterize as transaction-based compensation.

### 8.4 The trade to refuse

If Garrick offers equity in exchange for cash fee, hold roughly **$1 of
foregone fee for $3–4 of incremental profits interest** — it is illiquid,
subordinated, and 15+ years from realization. **Do not trade fee for equity at
par.**

### 8.5 The line not to cross

Do not use the equity grant to carry the economics of the success fee. If the
grant's size or vesting is set by reference to a financing closing, it can be
recharacterized as transaction-based compensation regardless of the label —
precisely the exposure the unlicensed period creates. Both templates carry
counsel notes anticipating this. The architecture works; loading the grant up
to substitute for the success fee breaks it.

## A.3 The seven engines, and what each one does

### 1. `readiness_checklist.py` — intake scoring (331 lines)

Ingests a document submission against all 272 items. Scores it exactly as the
DSA defines: **complete ÷ applicable**, with justified "Not Applicable"
excluded from *both* numerator and denominator.

**The design decision worth knowing:** N/A *without* written justification
counts as **incomplete**. Otherwise "not applicable" becomes a way to inflate
the score by declaring inconvenient items irrelevant. That is a one-line rule
that protects the integrity of the entire threshold.

Outputs a RAG board per process, item-by-item gap analysis naming every
missing document, and a binary Move Forward decision at 80%.

**On Horn Lake:** 57.4% AMBER, 116 named gaps, Move Forward does not issue.

### 2. `preflight.py` — structural viability (507 lines)

**The engine that answers the question the checklist cannot.**

Readiness asks *"does the sponsor have the documents?"* Preflight asks *"does
the credit actually work?"* These are orthogonal. A sponsor can deliver all
272 items, score 100%, and still be running a financing that cannot be sold at
any price — in which case the checklist has done nothing but document, in
exhaustive detail, why the deal fails.

So preflight **assumes the checklist is complete** and asks what remains.
Twelve structural trap detectors across three severities:

- **NO_GO** — cannot be done as contemplated. Walking in week one instead of
  month nine is the most valuable output this engine produces.
- **STRUCTURAL** — financeable, but not as structured.
- **WATCH** — disclose and monitor.

Detectors: fatal coverage, thin coverage, sub-IG coverage, tax-exempt
ineligibility on private-use property, missing conduit issuer, unseasoned
assessment security, pre-stabilization revenue risk, capitalized-interest
exhaustion, overleverage, demand concentration, series sizing, phasing
cascade.

Every trap states its own reasoning, cites the threshold it used, names the
evidence that fired it, and gives the fix. Missing inputs return
`cannot assess` rather than a guess.

**On Horn Lake:** RESTRUCTURE — four structural traps, led by the $275M
tax-exempt exposure.

### 3. `success_predictor.py` — where it stalls (367 lines)

Per-gate clearing probability across all ten arrangement gates, identifying
the single stall point and the critical-path items blocking the most gates.

**Three modeling errors were found and fixed by running the real deal:**

1. Missing a feasibility study scored identically to missing a conduit issuer.
   One you go buy; the other can kill the deal. That drove a financeable
   project to 0%. Procurable shortfalls now floor the gate.
2. Multiplying ten gate probabilities scored even a *flawless* project at 28%.
   Financings die from one binding constraint, not ten independent coin
   flips. Now blends weakest-link with average preparedness.
3. The headline reported only today's frozen parameters. It now reports as-is
   **and** what the deal reaches once procurable items are procured — which is
   the number an engagement is actually selling.

**On Horn Lake:** 38% as-is → 48% with procurable items in hand → 85% ceiling.
Stall at closing.

Enhancement and Rating heaviest, because that is where the work moves the

Preflight diagnoses. Stairway treats. **"Not financeable" is almost never the

## A.4 How the fee was actually derived

This is the part worth walking Garrick through, because it is the difference
between a quote and a derivation.

**Step 1 — Ingest the deal.** Program cost $1,405,000,000, six phases, 75% LTC
all-bond → **$1,053,750,000 bond authorization**, sized phase by phase.

**Step 2 — Score readiness.** 272 items → 57.4% AMBER → Move Forward does not
issue → **only Gate 1 is chargeable today, and it is refundable.** The fee
schedule is gated by the client's own document state.

**Step 3 — Run preflight.** Four structural traps → verdict RESTRUCTURE →
**this determines scope.** The $275M tax-exempt question is why Gate 5 (Bond
Counsel) matters on this deal specifically, and the cap-i gap is why Gate 2
(Capital Stack) carries a 22% weight.

**Step 4 — Predict the stall point.** 38% as-is, stalling at closing, with
permits and feasibility blocking three gates each. **This risk-adjusts the
fee** — gated fees collected as work lands are worth more per dollar than a
success fee years out at 38% odds. That asymmetry is the entire argument for
the pay-on-delivery structure.

**Step 5 — Size the fee per phase.** Tier by bond par (45 bp under $150M, 35 bp
above), allocate across ten gates by weight, compute hours and the implied
hourly rate.

**Step 6 — Split by licence status.** Development fees pre-license
($3,898,125 + $2,634,375 architecture = **$6,532,500 collectable now**),
success fees post-license (**$28,978,125**).

**Step 7 — Map the pathway.** 18 steps, $100K–$325K, 14.8–36.0 months — so the
engagement scope is priced against the work actually required, not a generic
timeline.

**Result: $35,510,625 — 3.37% of bond par.**

**The validation that matters:** 73 Holdings' own prospectus states a 3.625%
blended fee. We derived 3.37% independently, from the ground up, without
anchoring to that number. **We came in $2.7M under a figure Garrick has
already published to investors** — and we can show every step of how.

An earlier run on the nine-series $391.5M ladder produced $12,193,875 against
the prospectus's stated $12.20M — a **$6,125 difference** on a completely
independent derivation. Two convergences, two different bases. The method
holds.

## A.6 Defects found and fixed along the way

Running Josh's documents through real code surfaced problems that reading them
would not have.

**In the platform:**
- `routes/construction.py` returned the Convivial St. Petersburg fixture for
  **any** deal id — a real deal id silently got someone else's data.
- `bond_type_engine.py` was real, correct, and **never called** by the live
  pipeline. Every deal got a hardcoded default bond type.
- Grok was configured but calling a **retired model id**, so every request
  failed. A second bug meant the OpenRouter branch ignored the model argument
  entirely.
- `emma_seed_data.py` loaded 10 modeled bonds into the same store as real EMMA
  filings, stamped with a **fabricated `emma.msrb.org` URL**. Anything reading
  that store could not tell modeled from filed. Now honestly reports **0
  verified filings, 10 modeled**.

**In our own new engines, caught by running the real deal:**
- The fee optimizer **double-counted** placement gates into the gated pool,
  making every optimization prefer loading into success fees.
- Stairway compounded **alternative** steps as if mandatory — a bridgeable
  seasoning constraint read as an unavoidable political dependency.
  Feasibility scored 17% when the correct answer was 61%.
- The success predictor's three modeling errors described in A.3.

**In the deal documents:**
- Bond sizing stated three ways across two prospectus versions.
- NEST's fee stated three ways ($12.20M / 3.625% / 2.25%).
- Phase costs differing between the `.md` and HTML versions.

## A.7 What is honestly not done

Stated plainly so nothing here is oversold.

- **Nothing is calibrated.** Every threshold, hour estimate and pathway cost
  is `HAND_SET_PLANNING_ESTIMATE`. Zero verified EMMA filings against a
  30-filing minimum. The engines say so in their own output.
- **No self-learning.** Deliberately — a threshold fitted on zero closed deals
  is an assertion wearing a lab coat. The scaffolding is there; the data is not.
- **FRED, EDGAR and EMMA are not wired into preflight or stairway.** They are
  where the calibration will come from, and the ingestion has not been built.
- **Hour estimates are flat across phases** when they should taper with par.
- **The frontend escrow module** (`ClientDepositPlatform.tsx`) exists and is
  well-decomposed, but runs on demo data. The backend that should feed it now
  exists; they have not been connected.
- **The 2027A small-series economics** come out at $96/hour on the nine-series
  model — below cost.
