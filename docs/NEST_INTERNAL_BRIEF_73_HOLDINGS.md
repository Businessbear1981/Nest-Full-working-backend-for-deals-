## 4. THE $275M QUESTION — READ THIS BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE

The program assumes **$275M tax-exempt — 66% of the bond stack** on the 29%
sizing. The financed property is hotel, retail, entertainment, sports academy,
resort and commercial space, owned and operated by a **private developer**.

Tax-exempt treatment follows the **use of the financed property**, not who
issues the paper. Private business use of that kind is generally not
tax-exempt eligible absent a governmental issuer financing a qualifying public
purpose, or a 501(c)(3) borrower. Neither is established here — **no conduit
issuer has even been identified.**

Modeled coupons: tax-exempt 4.75%–5.75%. Taxable 144A 7.25%–11.5%.

**If we are right, a large part of this capital stack is mispriced by 250–575
basis points.** If we are wrong, we are about to tell a client to restructure
something that did not need it.

**This is a bond counsel question and it must be answered before we publish a
capital stack.** It is Q1 in the Grok research prompt. Do not let this reach
a buyer's counsel first — finding it ourselves is worth more to the
relationship than any fee on this list.

**One correction to our own engine, for the record:** our preflight classifies
"no conduit issuer identified" as NO_GO. Running the pathway showed that is
too harsh — restructuring fully taxable always exists. It changes the deal and
costs 250–575 bp, but it is not a dead end. We caught it in a test and
documented it rather than quietly changing the severity.

## 8. EQUITY — JOSH, THIS SECTION IS FOR YOU

The Equity Grant Letter as drafted has **two defects that can cost NEST cash.**

### 8.1 It sits behind everything, in a structure built not to distribute

The grant is a **profits interest under Rev. Proc. 93-27** in the Project SPV.
It pays only from residual cash — after debt service, after the rate covenant,
after DSRF replenishment, and only when the SPV actually **distributes**.

Garrick's own prospectus says the program recycles residual equity: *"$912M+
in free equity cycles through phases,"* *"Phase 1 stabilization releases
equity to fund Phase 2."* **A program engineered to reinvest residual cash is
a program engineered not to distribute.** A fully vested profits interest here
produces no cash, plausibly for 15+ years, until a REIT placement or phase
sale. It is a long-dated option on terminal value, not deferred compensation.

### 8.2 Phantom income — we would owe cash tax on money we never receive

This is written into the template. Section 1.2 adopts Rev. Proc. 2001-43,
under which NEST is treated as owner of the **entire** grant — explicitly
**including the unvested portion** — and must take into account its
distributive share of Company income in computing tax liability. Section 1.3
requires a protective §83(b) election.

Once the SPV allocates taxable income to NEST, **NEST owes cash tax on income
it has not received.** In a leveraged development SPV with covenant sweeps and
a stated equity-recycling policy, allocated income and distributed cash
diverge sharply and persistently.

**Do not sign without a mandatory tax distribution provision:**

> The Company shall distribute to each holder of a Granted Interest, no later
> than 75 days following the close of each fiscal year, an amount not less
> than the product of (a) the net taxable income allocated to such holder and
> (b) the highest combined marginal federal and state rate applicable to such
> holder. Such distributions shall be senior in priority to any reinvestment,
> capital recycling, or phase-funding application of Company cash.

This must go in **both** operating agreements — the SPV and the program parent.

### 8.3 The blended structure to actually propose

| Tranche | Entity | Size | Vests on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project | Phase SPV | 1.5–2.5% profits interest | That phase's development milestones (1/3, 1/3, 1/3) |
| Program | 73 Holdings parent | 0.5–1.0% profits interest | **Time**, 36 months from DSA signing — not on any closing |

Time-vesting at the program level is the cleanest position we can hold while
unlicensed. Time-vested equity for continuous advisory services is the hardest
thing to recharacterize as transaction-based compensation.

### 8.4 The trade to refuse

If Garrick offers equity in exchange for cash fee, hold roughly **$1 of
foregone fee for $3–4 of incremental profits interest** — reflecting that it
is illiquid, subordinated, and 15+ years from realization. **Do not trade fee
for equity at par.** That trade is where the current read of equity costs us
the most money.

### 8.5 The line not to cross

Do not use the equity grant to carry the economics of the success fee. If the
grant's size or vesting is set by reference to a financing closing, it can be
recharacterized as transaction-based compensation regardless of the label —
which is precisely the exposure the unlicensed period creates. Both templates
already carry counsel notes anticipating this. The architecture works;
loading the grant up to substitute for the success fee breaks it.

**Compare these on time-to-market, not just cost.** A 36-month remediation and
a 9-month restructure that reach the same capital are not equivalent.
